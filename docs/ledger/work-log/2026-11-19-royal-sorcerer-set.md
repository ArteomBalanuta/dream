## What was handed over, and what happened to it

A self-contained Python builder (`build_sorcerer.py`, numpy + Pillow) for a "Dark Fantasy Royal
Sorcerer Armor Set". It was run in isolation and does exactly what it claims: a valid 749 KB glTF
2.0, 1,620 triangles, a 26-bone armature, a 1024² PBR atlas, AO baked into COLOR_0 and an idle
animation. It is good work.

**The asset was not used.** Four properties of this engine make it unusable as a drop-in, and all four
are measured, not assumed:

1. it is skinned to its own 26-bone armature, while equipment here is unskinned parts drawn on the
   player's node matrices and the player model is a rigid hierarchy with no skin;
2. it is one merged suit, while this game has seven independently equippable slots;
3. its 1.96 m scale and A-pose arms are baked into vertex positions;
4. **62% of its albedo texels exceed 0.42 in some channel and 24% exceed 0.80** — this world pass has
   no tonemap and a gain reaching ~2.2, so its gold and most of its indigo would clip to flat white.

What was taken is the **design**, which maps almost one-to-one onto the shape library from the
sculpted-equipment wave. That is what having built that library is for.

## The one palette addition, and why

The design is indigo plate with gold trim. The palette had no indigo that was not nearly black
(`ClothCold` is (0.07, 0.078, 0.10)), so the frozen contract added **`ArmourMaterial.Indigo`** at
(0.095, 0.080, 0.185) — about (0.21, 0.17, 0.40) at the peak gain, so a clear deep indigo that never
clips — metallic 0.85, plate pattern, low grain because lacquer does not pit like bare iron.

`check_char.py` guarded this properly: on the contract commit alone it was RED with *"palette
materials never drawn by the clip gate: ['Indigo']"*, and it went green only when the first pieces
used the material. A palette entry nothing draws is a failure here, which is the right rule.

## Open items

- `dream/ui` is now **15.60 MB against the 16 MB budget** in `check_loader.py` (was 14.91). The next
  icon wave needs a compression pass first.
- The loot invariant pinned by `ShippedItemFootprintTests` is that every rollable item appears in both
  the wolf and dire-wolf tables, so these six had to go there. That means a level-1 wolf can drop the
  best armour in the game. It is consistent with how every other piece is handled, but it is a balance
  question somebody should decide deliberately rather than inherit from a test.
- The handoff's `build_sorcerer.py`, its GLB and its textures were deliberately **not** committed. The
  asset pipeline here requires original procedural art whose generator lives in `tools/ui-assets` and
  is gated byte-for-byte; a standalone script writing a 750 KB GLB into the repo satisfies none of
  that. The files stay as reference material outside the repo, and the technique was packaged as a
  separate skill instead.
